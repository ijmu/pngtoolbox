Format decision

When JPG is the right output

Photos for email, forms, or older systemsJPG is accepted everywhere and keeps files small — ideal when transparency isn't needed.
Web images where file size winsA quality-80 JPG is usually far smaller than the same image as PNG.
Logos, screenshots, and text-heavy graphicsStay with PNG — JPG artifacts blur sharp edges and small text.
Anything with transparent pixelsStay with PNG — JPG has no alpha channel, and transparency is filled with white.

Questions

WebP to JPG FAQ

Does converting WebP to JPG lose quality?

WebP photos are usually already lossy, and JPG adds one more compression pass. At quality 80–90 the difference is hard to see; the measured file size is shown so you can balance quality and size yourself.

What happens to transparent pixels in JPG output?

JPG has no alpha channel, so transparent areas are filled with white before encoding. If you need transparency, choose PNG output instead.

Why is my JPG larger than the original WebP?

WebP is simply a more efficient format for photos. A JPG at high quality can be larger than the WebP source — lower the quality slider if size matters more than fidelity.

Can I convert many WebP files to JPG at once?

Yes. Add a batch, choose JPG as the output format, and download every result individually or as one ZIP archive.
