Compatibility

When apps still fail to open WebP

In current browsers, WebP compatibility is effectively solved. Outside browsers, older software, print workflows and upload portals still create predictable walls. Here is the 2026 checklist and the local fix.

By PNGToolbox EditorialPublished 2026-11-07Last reviewed 2026-11-07How we test

At a glance

  • Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Opera render WebP natively; Safari added full support in version 14.
  • Failures now cluster in legacy desktop software, print/RIP workflows and whitelist-based upload portals.
  • Convert locally to PNG for graphics, text and transparency; convert to JPG for photographs and the broadest form compatibility.
  • Office and other desktop apps may support WebP depending on the exact application version, update channel and administrator policy; test the target environment before sending.

The short answer

For a modern website, WebP is a safe choice. For a file leaving your own controlled environment, compatibility depends on the exact app and policy. A portal that accepts only JPG and PNG will reject WebP regardless of which browser you use.

Where WebP still breaks

Office and desktop apps

Office and other desktop apps may or may not include a WebP decoder. Compatibility depends on the exact application version, update channel and administrator policy. Test in the target environment before sending; if the recipient cannot open the file, send PNG or JPG instead.

Print shops and RIP workflows

Print pipelines commonly accept TIFF, JPEG, PDF and sometimes PNG. WebP support varies by RIP and operator. Ask the shop; PNG is the safer choice for line art and text, while high-quality JPG suits photographs.

Government and enterprise upload portals

The most common failure is an old extension and MIME whitelist. Convert the file locally and retry; changing browsers cannot change the portal's accepted-format policy.

Old Android and desktop viewers

WebP support arrived in stages on Android: lossy decoding at 4.0 and lossless/alpha support at 4.2. Legacy viewers and clean offline Windows installations can also lack a decoder or extension.

Why these failures persist

  1. Decoders are shipped with applications or operating systems. Slow-release software may never gain a newer codec.
  2. Upload validation is a deliberate whitelist. A portal can reject a valid image because its downstream systems accept only a short list of formats.

The local escape routes

  • PNG: lossless output for screenshots, documents, text and transparency.
  • JPG: smaller output for photographs and the broadest compatibility.
  • Keep WebP: reasonable only when you control every recipient and application.

FAQ

Is WebP still worth using in 2026?

Yes for web delivery and controlled modern environments. Use JPG or PNG when a file must pass through unknown software, a portal or a print shop.

Why won't Word open my WebP?

The installed app may not include a WebP decoder. Check the exact target environment and convert to PNG or JPG if it cannot open the file.

Does converting WebP to PNG add quality?

No. It preserves the decoded pixels; it cannot restore detail already lost by lossy WebP encoding.
