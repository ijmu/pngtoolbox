Output choice

PNG or JPG for the batch?

Choose PNGWhen files contain text, logos, screenshots, or transparency — or when the destination accepts any format and you want lossless quality. Output keeps an alpha channel and every pixel exact.
Choose JPGWhen the batch is photographic and size matters: product photos, blog images, gallery shots. Quality 85 is a good starting point — big savings, little visible loss. Transparent areas become white.

Questions

Batch conversion FAQ

How many WebP files can I convert at once?

There is no artificial limit. Select or drop as many files as you need — very large batches process slower on low-memory devices, but nothing is uploaded or queued on a server.

Do converted files keep their original names?

Yes — each output keeps its source filename with the extension changed to .png, so a batch of photo-1.webp, photo-2.webp, ... becomes photo-1.png, photo-2.png, ... in the ZIP.

Will PNG conversion keep my transparency?

Yes. Transparent pixels in the WebP source are preserved in the PNG output. If you convert to JPG instead, transparency is filled with white because JPG cannot store an alpha channel.

Why are my WebP files being converted to larger PNGs?

PNG is lossless: it stores every pixel exactly, while WebP compresses more aggressively. Larger file size is the normal price of lossless, universally compatible output.

Are my files uploaded while converting?

No. Decoding, conversion, and ZIP packaging all run locally in your browser. Your files never leave your device.
