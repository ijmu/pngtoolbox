PNG for ecommerce images: the right jobs and the wrong ones

Store owners usually meet PNG through a requirement — a transparent cutout, an exact white background, a marketplace spec. Knowing which listings genuinely need it saves a great deal of page weight.

By PNGToolbox Editorial Published 2026-10-22 Last reviewed 2026-10-22 How we test

At a glance

  • PNG earns its size on images with text, flat colour or transparency: logos, badges, spec sheets, cutouts.
  • Product photographs on a white sweep rarely need PNG; a JPEG or WebP at a sane quality is an order of magnitude smaller and indistinguishable in a grid thumbnail.
  • Marketplace rules differ: some require a plain white background rather than transparency, and several warn against digitally altered images.
  • The largest page-speed gain in most catalogues comes from resizing, not from format: serving 1600 px images into 400 px slots wastes bandwidth whatever the format.

The three legitimate uses

Cutouts that must overlay a page. A product with no background, placed on a coloured panel or a promotional banner, needs real transparency. PNG's alpha is exact, edges stay clean, and the file is the only copy that can be re-composited without a matte colour baked in.

Images containing text. Badges, size charts, care labels, screenshots of the product's own app or software, and any listing graphic with typography in it. This is the case where lossy formats fail visibly, and it is not a matter of taste: our measured UI screenshot grew from 170.9 KB to 752 KB as JPEG across the quality range while PSNR stayed flat at 26.7 dB, because the encoder spent its budget approximating glyph edges.

Flat-colour artwork. Line illustrations, diagrams, icon-based infographics. Where the palette is small, PNG in indexed colour is genuinely small — a few kilobytes for an image that would be several times larger as a photograph-derived JPEG — because large uniform regions cost almost nothing to describe.

The uses that cost you page weight

Photographic product shots. A 1200 x 1200 studio photo stored as PNG can easily be over a megabyte. The same image as a WebP or JPEG at quality 80 is often under 100 KB. In a catalogue grid viewed on a phone, the difference is invisible and the bandwidth difference is tenfold.

Hero images and lifestyle photography. Same arithmetic. Where the destination accepts a lossy format, a PNG is paying for detail that will be scaled down and then recompressed by the platform anyway.

Anything the platform will re-encode. If the marketplace pipeline converts your upload regardless, hand it the best-quality source at a sane size and let its own encoder handle the output — but do check the platform's stated limits first, since some reject files above a byte threshold.

White background or transparency?

These are not interchangeable, and marketplaces treat them differently:

Requirement What to supply Note
"Plain white background" An image photographed or composited on pure white (RGB 255,255,255) Supplying a cutout on a white field is not the same thing; visible edge artefacts are a common rejection reason
"Transparent background accepted" PNG or WebP with an alpha channel Useful for banners and composites; not accepted by every listing type
"No digital alteration" An image edited only by cropping and resizing Many identification and official-photo rules say this explicitly — check before applying a background remover

The practical approach: read the requirement as written. If it says white, produce white. If it says transparent, keep the alpha. If it forbids alteration, restrict edits to crop and size.

Sizing is the bigger lever

A catalogue's page weight is decided mostly by how many pixels it ships, not by which format those pixels are in. Three habits cover most of the ground:

  1. Export at the size the layout needs. A 400 px thumbnail slot does not need a 2000 px source. Halving width and height removes three quarters of the pixel data before any format question arises.
  2. One image per slot. Serving a large hero and scaling it in CSS ships the full bytes to every visitor.
  3. Keep the master. Store the largest, best-quality version as the source of truth, and generate delivery sizes from it — never from an already-compressed derivative.

Format choice comes after that. For photographs, a lossy format at moderate quality; for text and cutouts, PNG, ideally with a palette where the colours allow it.

The cutout workflow, and its limits

For the images that genuinely need transparency, a colour-based removal is the fastest route, and the tool behind this guide runs entirely in the browser. It works well on plain, evenly lit backgrounds; it fails on hair, fur, glass, shadows and gradients, where a visible halo or a hard-edged silhouette appears. In those cases the honest options are a manual mask in an editor or a re-shoot against a cleaner backdrop.

Two checks before publishing a cutout: view it over both a white and a dark background (a halo that is invisible on white often shows on dark), and confirm that the alpha really survived the export — JPEG has no alpha channel at all, so a transparent PNG saved as JPEG will be flattened onto a matte colour with no warning.

A quick decision list

Image Format Why
Studio photo, white sweep, listing requires white JPEG or WebP, quality 78-85 Lossy is fine; size is what matters
Product cutout over a coloured banner PNG Exact alpha, no matte baked in
Logo, badge or icon PNG with a reduced palette, or WebP lossless Flat colours compress well losslessly
Size chart or care-label graphic with text PNG Lossy artefacts on text are unmissable
Lifestyle photograph WebP or JPEG Photographic content, lossy delivery
Spec sheet generated as an image PNG, sized to fit the slot Text fidelity matters more than bytes
